OWNER-PROXY REVIEW — assyria cycle
Verdict: approve-with-patch

The core worry — hollow-but-well-formed upgrades — does not materialize. Every one of the ten passages actually tells its history rather than gesturing at it: the Amarna passage itemizes the gifts ("two chariots, two horses, and a ring of lapis lazuli") and lands the diplomatic consequence via Burnaburiash's protest; the banquet-stele paragraph risks a long enumeration but earns it, because the list is the inscription's own voice and the closing sentence converts it into an argument ("only a state that could marshal herds, granaries, and vineyards on this scale... could credibly claim to rule the region at all"); the Sargon-death paragraph names Gurdî of Tabal and turns the "Sin of Sargon" into a real story of a son "publicly disowning a father"; Damqi and Sinqisha-amur give the ritual and oracle material attested human faces. Register matches the chapter and the Sumer standard — the Bronze-Age-collapse "continuity state" passage in particular is exactly the thesis-scale sentence the brief asked for, and the Scythian/Herodotus passage in `as-fall` is the book's calibration doctrine done properly. I found no invented texture: the banquet menu, the 920 lions, the Arvad voyage, the substitute-queen detail all map onto the attested corpora, and the referee's honeypot spot-checks hold up against my own knowledge.

Three small defects survived the referee, all in the accuracy/seam class, all patchable without reopening the writer. First, the Amarna passage claims the letters are "among the oldest surviving Assyrian voices in the historical record at all" — the chapter's own Merchant Centuries act, two sections earlier, reads twenty thousand Assyrian tablets from Kanesh written five centuries before Ashur-uballit. This is an internal contradiction a careful reader will catch. Second, the same passage says Burnaburiash "wrote to Akhenaten in protest": the protest letter is EA 9 (the referee already retargeted the reference to EA 9), and EA 9's addressee "Nibhurrereya" is most commonly identified as Tutankhamun, not Akhenaten — the prose still carries the same misattribution the referee fixed only in the ref label. Third, a seam: the Ashurbanipal paragraph in `as-sargonids` still carries its old inline summary of the civil war ("dead, by most accounts, in his own burning palace — told in fuller, harder detail... Babylonia (I·3)"), and the new Brothers' War card sits immediately below telling the same events in full, ending with the same deferral to Babylonia. The reader gets the burning-palace death twice and the I·3 hand-off twice within one screen — the paragraph was plainly written before the card existed and was not trimmed when the card arrived.

On duplication against Babylonia I·3 itself, I read `bb-assyrian` ("Burned by Brothers") side by side with the new card and concur with the referee that the split holds: the facts necessarily overlap, but the card's distinctive material — Assyrian officials "answering to Nineveh," the revolt as "the predictable result of a decade spent treating a king as a subordinate governor," and above all the strategic cost ("two years of strength at the exact moment Cimmerian and Scythian pressure was building") — is imperial-frame analysis Babylonia does not carry. The internal paragraph-vs-card redundancy above is the real duplication defect, not the cross-chapter one.

Patches (bounded; referee-applicable, all in `master/content/era-1.html`):
1. In the `as-middle` Amarna passage, delete the clause ", and among the oldest surviving Assyrian voices in the historical record at all" (sentence then ends "...the earliest surviving contact between Assyria and Egypt."), and change "Babylon's king Burnaburiash II wrote to Akhenaten in protest" to "Babylon's king Burnaburiash II wrote to the Egyptian court in protest" (EA 9's addressee identification is disputed and most likely Tutankhamun; depersonalizing is the safe fix).
2. In the `as-sargonids` Ashurbanipal paragraph, replace "a devastating civil war, beginning in 652 BCE, that ended in 648 with Babylon starved into submission and Shamash-shum-ukin dead, by most accounts, in his own burning palace — told in fuller, harder detail from Babylon's own side in this Chronicle's <a data-goto="babylonia" href="#">Babylonia (I·3)</a>." with "a devastating civil war, beginning in 652 BCE, whose full course the panel just below follows." — keeping the existing `[14]` cite. The card retains both the full narration and the Babylonia deferral.
3. In the continuity-state passage (`as-middle`), soften "making Assyria, alone among the great Late Bronze Age powers, something close to a continuity state" to "making Assyria, more nearly than any other great power of its age, a continuity state" — Egypt also survived the collapse as a functioning state (the cited ACOUP essay says so itself), so "alone" overclaims even under the b-inf badge.
